Roundup 01 · Clutter

15 desk accessories that actually cut clutter

Clutter is a systems problem, not just a storage problem. This guide focuses on accessories that give the things on your desk a defined home, move bulky items off the surface, or make cables easier to control — with the tradeoff called out so you can choose deliberately.

As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases. Some links below are affiliate links — you pay the same price, and it helps support Desk Atlas. Recommendations are selected for usefulness, with the tradeoffs explained clearly.

Surface organizers

Get things off the desktop and into a home. Footprint is the whole game here.

01

Bamboo monitor stand with drawer

Raises the screen while creating a compact home for pens, a phone and small desk items underneath.

Tradeoff The drawer is shallow; check its dimensions before buying.

02

Under-desk headphone hook

Moves headphones from the desktop to unused space under the desk and keeps them easy to grab.

Tradeoff Adhesive versions need a clean, suitable surface and may not suit heavy headsets.

03

Slim pen and tool tray

A low tray keeps frequently used small items visible without the footprint of a deep organizer.

Tradeoff Less capacity means you have to be selective about what stays on the desk.

04

Cable management box

Hides a power strip and excess cable length so the visual mess disappears from the floor or desktop.

Tradeoff A box takes more physical space than a simple cable tray.

Cable control

The biggest visual-clutter win for most setups. Control the cables before adding more storage.

05

Under-desk cable tray

Lifts a power strip, charger bricks and loose cable from the floor into one hidden channel beneath the desk.

Tradeoff Check mounting method and desk thickness before choosing a model.

06

Adhesive cable clips

Keeps the one or two cables that must reach the desktop routed exactly where you need them.

Tradeoff Adhesive performance varies by surface and is harder to reposition cleanly.

07

Cable sleeve wrap

Bundles several visible cables into one cleaner run when hiding them completely isn't practical.

Tradeoff Adding or removing a cable later can be fiddly.

08

Reusable hook-and-loop cable ties

A simple way to shorten excess cable and keep bundles adjustable when your setup changes.

Tradeoff They tidy cables but do not hide them, so routing still matters.

Screen and light

Reclaim desk surface by moving hardware upward or choosing accessories with a smaller footprint.

09

Single monitor arm

Frees the footprint under the screen and gives you more control over monitor position.

Tradeoff Check VESA compatibility, desk thickness and available depth before buying.

10

Monitor light bar

Places task lighting above the monitor instead of occupying a corner of the desktop.

Tradeoff Compatibility and glare control vary by monitor shape.

11

Laptop stand

Gets a laptop off the desk and can create a cleaner two-screen arrangement.

Tradeoff A fixed stand can reduce flexibility if you frequently switch between seated and standing positions.

Small-item control

The final layer: give the little things a home so they stop becoming visual noise.

12

Desk drawer organizer

Moves small accessories, adapters and stationery below the main visual field while keeping them sorted.

Tradeoff It only helps if the drawer itself is easy to reach and sized for your desk.

13

Vertical document holder

Keeps notebooks and papers upright instead of spreading them across the working surface.

Tradeoff Vertical storage is less useful if you rarely keep paper at your desk.

14

Charging station organizer

Gives phones, watches and small devices one charging location instead of creating several loose cable nests.

Tradeoff Works best when your devices use compatible charging hardware.

15

Desk mat with cable channel

Defines the main working area while some designs provide a cleaner route for charging or peripheral cables.

Tradeoff A large mat uses more surface area, so measure a small desk first.

If you only buy three

  1. Start with an under-desk cable tray if cables are your biggest source of clutter.
  2. Add a monitor arm if your screen is taking up valuable desk surface.
  3. Use a small-item organizer only after the bigger sources of clutter are under control.
